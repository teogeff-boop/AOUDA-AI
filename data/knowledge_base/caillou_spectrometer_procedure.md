# Operational Procedures for Synthetic Raman Spectrometer 'Caillou' (AMADEE EVA)

## Hardware Checklist
- Caillou instrument
- Correct handle installed for the mission (AA, Rover, or Drone)
- Dedicated support box
- Remote control interface

## Pre-EVA Preparation (Habitat)
- Step 1: Charge the internal battery. Connect via USB-C port on the main body (Duration: 2 hours).
- Step 2: Select the correct handle for AA, Rover, or Drone (Duration: 30 seconds).
- Step 3: Slide and lock the handle. Ensure mechanical connection is secure (Duration: 30 seconds).
- Step 4: Place instrument inside the support box. Ensure instrument is correctly seated (Duration: 30 seconds).

## Rover Deployment Procedure
- Step 1: Position rover arm above support box using arm camera to align with instrument handle (Duration: 3 minutes).
- Step 2: Grip and lift instrument. Powers on instantly upon removal from support box (Duration: 30 seconds).
- Step 3: Transport to area of interest.

## Laser Targeting Procedure
- Step 4: Activate laser beam via remote command interface (Duration: 10 seconds).
- Step 5: Align laser with sample. Adjust rover arm until laser dot is on target (Duration: 1 minute).
- Step 6: Capture context picture via remote command interface (Duration: 1 minute).

## Spectrometer Measurement Procedure
- Step 7: Lower instrument to ground. Move until skirt makes contact with ground to ensure darkness (Duration: 1 minute).
- Step 8: Trigger measurement via remote command interface and wait for data generation (Duration: 30 seconds).

## Recovery and Additional Measurement Decision
- Step 9: Lift device back up. Clear sample area before moving rover (Duration: 1 minute).
- Step 10: Additional measurements decision point. If YES: Move rover to next target and return to Step 4. If NO: Proceed to Step 11.

## End of EVA Procedure
- Step 11: Drive back to habitat. Navigate rover to garage or docking area.
- Step 12: Store Caillou back in support box. Instrument powers off automatically. Confirm End of Mission (Duration: 1 minute).
